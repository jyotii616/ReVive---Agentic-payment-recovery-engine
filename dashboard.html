Baseline vs. ReVive

Same 500 failed transactions, same total exposure. The baseline is one generic retry email sent 24 hours later, which is what most merchants run today. ReVive runs each failure through diagnosis, timing, channel, a compliance check, and a trained recovery-probability model.

02

Working under a real ops budget

No team can chase every failed payment. This ranks every playbook by expected value (amount times probability times confidence) and only works the top 35% of cases, the same way a small ops team actually would on day one.

03

The Regulator's audit trail

Every playbook the swarm produces gets checked against contact-frequency, consent, and risk-hold rules before it can go out. Nothing gets sent that the Regulator has not signed off on.

04

Learning the right incentive over time

The Negotiator's incentive choice runs as a small epsilon-greedy bandit across 10 simulated days: no incentive, a small cashback, or an EMI conversion offer. It starts guessing and converges on whichever arm actually pays off, without being told the answer up front.

05

The swarm

Five narrow agents, kept separate on purpose so a human reviewer can follow the reasoning instead of trusting one score.

Diagnosis

The Coroner

Classifies the failure as transient, friction, or structural, since a bank timeout and an insufficient-funds decline need different responses.

Timing

The Psychologist

Decides when to re-approach: immediately while intent is fresh, next morning if it died at 2am, or timed to a likely payday window.

Approach

The Negotiator

Picks the channel, decides if an incentive is worth the margin, and drafts the actual message.

Compliance

The Regulator

Audits every proposed playbook against contact caps and risk-hold rules, and can block a send outright.

Verdict

The Judge

Combines all four opinions with a trained model into one ranked, explainable playbook.
